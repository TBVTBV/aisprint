Access

01 · 2 requirements

The lightest gate a hospital would accept. A caregiver holding an infant will not create an account.

  1. 01

    No account creation

    Entry is a link. The only gate is a phone number plus a one-time code, faked in the prototype so any number and any code are accepted.

  2. 02

    The session persists

    A returning caregiver lands where they left off rather than back at the gate.

The named ask

02 · 6 requirements

This is the hypothesis. A generic upload box is the checklist the clinic already tried, and it lapsed.

  1. 03

    Documents are specifically namedcore

    Each item carries a name, a date and a source. Never a generic upload box. This is the one thing that separates this product from the checklist that already failed.

  2. 04

    Each item says why the clinician needs it

    One plain line per item, on the card.

  3. 05

    At least one item is added at runtimecore

    Triggered by an earlier answer, and the card says why it appeared. This is the adaptivity the demo turns on.

  4. 06

    "I don't have it" is a normal outcome

    It has its own follow-up. Not a failure state, no scolding copy, no red.

  5. 07

    Attaching works two ways

    Choosing a file from the phone, and photographing a page, with capture instructions shown before the camera opens.

  6. 08

    Extracted content is labelled, flagged and editable

    Anything the app reads from a document is marked as machine-read, flagged where it is unsure, and correctable. At least one field must demonstrate the uncertain state. Do not remove it to make the demo look cleaner.

The question engine

03 · 6 requirements

Short and adaptive, or it becomes the comprehensive questionnaire that ideation killed.

  1. 09

    Data-driven and progressively disclosedcore

    The tree decides what comes next from what has already been answered. Questions are data, not markup.

  2. 10

    Up to nine questions on any single path

    Never more. If a branch would push a path past nine, cut the branch rather than the cap.

  3. 11

    Follow-ups reveal inline

    Under their parent question, never as a new screen.

  4. 12

    Every question carries an explicit "I don't know"core

    Stored as a value that is distinct from skipped and from unanswered. Styled as a normal choice, not a smaller or greyer one.

  5. 13

    Recall questions carry a confidence marker

    Sure, roughly, guessing. Shown in words on the review screen, so the clinician knows where to spend the check they will do anyway.

  6. 14

    Six input types work

    Single select, multi select, bucketed ranges, scale, approximate date, free text.

Completion and honesty

04 · 6 requirements

Published completion for pre-visit questionnaires sits near half. Design against abandonment, not for perfection.

  1. 15

    Submit is available from the second step and never disabledcore

    A partial form reaches review and sends. Leaving it unfinished is a success state.

  2. 16

    Gaps shown as two separate counters

    Don't knows and passed on, kept apart and framed as useful information for the clinician rather than as omissions.

  3. 17

    All counts computed at runtime

    Never hard-coded. If two builds disagree on a number, the counting rule is wrong, not the number.

  4. 18

    Review rows are tappable to edit

    Jump back to the question, change it, return to review with state preserved.

  5. 19

    The app never states a conclusion about the patientgate

    It collects and routes. Nothing more. This is a guardrail, not a preference.

  6. 20

    Fictional-data notice and storage disclaimer both presentgate

    Pediatric hospital context. Nothing here is a real record, and the caregiver is told where the answers live.

Mechanics

05 · 5 requirements

The caregiver is on a phone, in Hebrew, holding an infant. Everything here follows from that.

  1. 21

    Phone-first layout

    Designed at phone width, and shown inside a device frame on wider viewports so the demo reads correctly on a laptop and a projector.

  2. 22

    Hebrew by default, with full RTLcore

    English available from the menu, switchable from any screen without losing state. A real direction flip, not a text swap.

  3. 23

    Save, close, reopen and resume

    Where the caregiver left off. She will not do this in one sitting.

  4. 24

    Reschedule reachable from the document step and the menu

    Stating on screen that answers are kept and the named list stays the same.

  5. 25

    Single self-contained HTML file

    No build step, no CDN, no network at runtime, no dead ends on the happy path.

Deliberately out of P0

06 · 6 items

Not cut for lack of value. Cut so the P0 build lands. Each should be designed around rather than designed out.

CutWhy, and what to preserve
Error handling and recovery statesWrong codes, failed reads, unsupported files, offline. Fail soft and silently rather than spending a screen on messages nobody reads in a demo.
Voice inputProbably the highest-value P1 item for a Hebrew-speaking clinic. A convincing fake needs a recorder, a waveform, a timer and a correction pass, which is a screen's work for one optional question.
The post-visit receiptThe reciprocity mechanism, and the reason a second form ever gets filled. Keep an opened field per document in the data model so it can be added without a rewrite.
Real authenticationFaked end to end. No server, no verification, no account.
Real camera accessThe file picker fallback is an acceptable P0 outcome, not a degradation.
Unused question branchesSpecified in the PRD, built only if time allows. The demo path is what has to be flawless.
