How to read this: the same evidence rule, applied to competitors

The Session 2 files separate real material from synthetic at note level and let you subtract the synthetic half to see what stands. This file does the same thing to competitive research. A vendor's own claim about its product is the synthetic material of a competitive scan: it is useful, it is not evidence, and it carries the hatch. Every claim below is coded by where it came from, at the point of use.

Peer-reviewed

Journal article, RCT, systematic review, validated instrument. Counted as evidence.

Official

Statute, regulator, ministry, standards body, government measure specification. Counted as evidence.

Press

Journalism, trade press, analyst report. Counted with caution; independent of the vendor but not peer-reviewed.

Vendor claim

The company's own site or press release. Hatched. Never counted as evidence, only as a statement of intent.

Unverified

We looked and could not confirm it. Named here rather than quietly dropped, so nobody re-researches it.

The honesty condition

Three findings in this brief are bad news for our concept and are stated as such in section 3. A comparison in which we win everywhere is not credible in front of two practising pulmonologists. Where a competitor is genuinely ahead, it says so.

The landscape on the two axes our own constraint creates

Insight I2 says the physicians will not accept another reader's conclusion. That gives the horizontal axis: does a product hand you a conclusion, or hand you the raw artefact? Insight I3 says the holes sit on organisational boundaries, which the record by definition cannot reach. That gives the vertical: does a product work the existing record, or capture what was never recorded? Our three directions sit in the top-right and bottom-right. Almost the entire funded market sits on the left.

    Where we are already beaten, and by how much

    Four places where a funded company has already built what we described, and three published numbers that undercut a claim in our ideation file. These are the slides a mentor will find on their own if we do not put them here first.

    Ambience Patient Summary Direct

    Pre-visit chart summaries, specialty-specific, pulling from the EHR and from Epic Care Everywhere, analysing 18 months of history, and linking directly to source notes so physicians can drill downS12. That is Direction A's sentence with two words changed. Announced 24 June 2025; the company raised a $243M Series C on 29 July 2025 at a $1.25B valuation, with 40+ US health systemsS13.

    What this kills. "Click through to the raw artefact" is not a differentiator. Provenance-linked summarisation is now table stakes across Epic Art, MEDITECH with Google, Abridge Linked Evidence and Abstractive Health. If our Initial Solution Concept leads on click-to-source, it is describing a 2025 feature.

    What survives. Ambience summarises and then lets you check. We refuse to summarise at all. That is a real difference, it is the one our physicians actually asked for, and nobody funded is defending it, because "we deliberately produce less output" is a hard thing to sell to a US health system and an easy thing to defend to a pulmonologist.

    Epic Direct

    Epic already ships the navigation layer we are proposing: Chart Review, Chart Search, Synopsis, and a Lifetime visual timeline of visits, admissions, problems and medicationsS9. On top of it, Insights summarises the chart for visit preparation and was used over 16 million times a month as of February 2026, roughly triple its November 2025 usageS10. 85% of Epic customers now use Epic AIS11.

    What this kills. In any Epic hospital, our direction is a zero-procurement default away from being redundant. It also means our physicians' stated preference is diverging from the market's revealed preference: KLAS 2026 found 51% of physicians already use AI for patient-history summariesS14.

    What survives. Schneider does not run Epic. Israeli hospitals run Chameleon by Elad Health, per the vendor's own reference listS6. The Epic AI stack is not the competitor in our room. That is a deployment accident, not a moat, and we should say so rather than pretend it is strategy.

    Health Note Direct

    Direction B assumes nobody collects clinical history before the visit. That is wrong. Health Note runs conversational text-based pre-visit clinical intake that populates history of present illness, chief complaint, family, medical and social history and medication review into discrete EHR fields, integrated with Epic, Athena, Veradigm and eClinicalWorksS15. It raised $17M in July 2022 with Cedars-Sinai, Northwell and UnityPoint as investorsS16, and lists Pediatric Associates as a customer.

    What this kills. Any sentence in our concept beginning "no one asks families in advance". Phreesia is larger still, processes around one in seven US visits, and already ships validated pediatric instruments including M-CHAT-R, ASQ-3 and PSC-17S17.

    What survives. Health Note lists eight specialties and pulmonology is not one of themS15. Both are long adaptive questionnaires optimised for note volume, which is the opposite of a two-question ask. And neither operates in Israel, in Hebrew, or with a "don't know" that is recorded rather than blank.

    The four numbers that undercut us Evidence

    1 · The ceiling on time saved is about eighteen seconds

    NYU Langone ran a six-month pre and post study of an EHR-integrated GPT-4 summarisation tool across 22 clinicians and roughly 16,000 visits. Average chart review time fell from 3:22 to 3:04. Clinicians with a higher baseline saved more, up to 49 seconds. Also: 22.7% of providers stopped reading full notes because the summary felt sufficient, which the authors flag as an overreliance riskS18.

    So what. Our north-star KPI cannot be minutes saved. State-of-the-art moved it by seconds. The defensible claim is what the physician finds, not how fast.

    2 · Access alone does not change behaviour

    Across eight Atlanta health systems, 80.4% of clinicians used the health information exchange at least daily, and 89.2% still ordered a duplicate test in the previous month because they could not reach a previous result. The top barrier was "needed information unavailable" at 66.4%; lack of awareness was only 11.7%S19. Separately, each additional hour of documentation was associated with a 7.1% reduction in the likelihood of accessing outside records at allS20.

    So what. This is the strongest evidence for our diagnosis and against our assumption. The gap is availability and findability, not speed. Building the route does not create the time to walk it.

    3 · Pre-visit questionnaires can make the visit longer

    A randomised trial of a self-administered pre-visit questionnaire found intervention visits ran 6.8 minutes longer (95% CI 0.4 to 13.2), covered 30% more diagnoses, and patient satisfaction tended to be lowerS21. Our ideation file already cites this to kill the comprehensive questionnaire, and it applies to Direction B too unless the ask stays genuinely short.

    4 · The delivery rail matters more than we assumed

    Electronic pre-visit questionnaires delivered by patient portal reached 3% completion in a safety-net clinic against 10% in a non-safety-net clinicS22. A tablet in the waiting room reached 45.7% overall in an asthma studyS23. In a mixed-language hospital population, portal delivery is not a neutral choice, it is a filter.

    What survives. All four numbers point the same way, and it is not away from our concept. They point away from speed as the claim and toward completeness and honesty as the claim. A boundary map and a recorded "don't know" are answers to "needed information unavailable, 66.4%". A two-question SMS is an answer to both the 6.8 minutes and the 3%.

    What survives the scan: six positions nobody currently holds

    Each of these was searched for specifically. Where we found nothing, that is recorded as "searched, not located", which is not the same as proof that nothing exists. Evidence strength is marked, and two of the six are thin.

    1 · The boundary map: stating what could not be reached

    Strong evidence Direction A

    We searched for a clinical product that displays what it could not retrieve and found none. The nearest neighbours are academic data-completeness methods and legal-sector record-gap catching. Every aggregator, from Epic Happy Together to Zus to Ofek, tells you what it has and is silent about what it missed.

    Why it holds up. The single largest barrier clinicians report is "needed information unavailable" at 66.4%S19. Naming the hole is a direct answer to the most-cited complaint in the literature, and it is the one thing a system can do honestly when it cannot fetch the document. It also converts an unknown into a named list a family or a secretary can act on, which is what wires Direction A to Directions B and C.

    Why the boundary is unstable, which makes it more defensible rather than less. Particle Health's antitrust case against Epic survived a motion to dismiss in September 2025 and went to discovery on monopolisation claimsS24. What a system is permitted to reach turns on purpose-of-use assertions and on the largest vendor's reading of them. A map of the boundary is durable precisely because the boundary keeps moving.

    2 · "I don't know" stored as a clinical value

    Strong evidence Direction B

    This is the cleanest gap in the brief, because the data model already exists and no product uses it. HL7's NullFlavor vocabulary, current in HL7 Terminology v6.5.0, distinguishes ASKU, "information was sought but not found, e.g. patient was asked but didn't know", from NASK, "this information has not been sought"S25. That distinction is Direction B's thesis, already standardised and encodable in FHIR.

    We searched for an intake product that surfaces "I don't know" as a first-class answer and stores it as ASKU rather than leaving a blank. We found none. The gap is not the standard, it is that every intake product treats a non-answer as an absence.

    Where the literature pushes back, honestly. Survey methods research is split. Prompting respondents to use the scale cut "don't know" responses from 55.7% to 19.6% and produced stronger construct correlationsS26. But "don't know" responses are systematically more likely among marginalised groups, predicted by lower health literacy and lower education, and dismissing them threatens the validity and generalisability of the researchS27. The reconciliation the evidence actually supports: use the confidence dial as the prompt, so "roughly" and "guessing" capture a value plus its uncertainty, and reserve a recorded "I don't know" for genuine non-knowledge.

    3 · The receipt: showing the family which answers were used

    Thin evidence, novel Direction B

    Searched specifically for portal read-receipts on questionnaire responses, PROM acknowledgement features and closing-the-loop on patient-submitted data. Nothing found. This appears to be the most genuinely novel sub-concept we hold.

    The supporting literature is suggestive, not conclusive. Patients may lose motivation to complete patient-reported measures over time if results are not discussed or used clinically, and valued having the significance of questions and results explainedS28. A realist synthesis reports more patients found the measure easy and helpful for remembering than felt the doctor had used the informationS29. Neither establishes that a visible receipt improves subsequent completion. That causal link is unproven and we should not claim it.

    The risk we are choosing

    A truthful receipt will sometimes read "the doctor opened none of your answers." In the one study with a number, provider responses appeared in the visit note for 70% and 62% of completed questionnaires across two clinic typesS22, meaning roughly a third left no trace. Ship the receipt only with a mechanism that raises the chance the answers get used, or it becomes a machine for documenting neglect.

    4 · Refusing to summarise, on purpose

    Strong evidence Direction A

    Every funded player in this space summarises. The differentiation race has moved to citing the summary, not to declining to make one. Epic Art references the notes, Ambience links to source notes, MEDITECH with Google grounds summaries to note sections, Abridge ships Linked Evidence, Abstractive Health offers sentence-level provenance, Zus traces every input to its source.

    The evidence for refusal is better than the market implies. Errors of omission, not hallucination, are the dominant failure. A UC San Diego Health study of Epic-integrated GPT-4 note summarisation logged 46 reports of missing information against 5 of hallucination, and concluded summaries should be a starting point when time is short but not a replacement for manual chart reviewS30. A separate framework study measured a 1.47% hallucination rate against a 3.45% omission rate, with hallucinations far more likely to be major errorsS31. And the NYU study found nearly a quarter of clinicians stopped opening full notesS18.

    Our concept 20, the Devil's Advocate, whose only permitted output is doubt, sits precisely here. It was marked deliberately weird in ideation. On this evidence it is the most defensible weird idea we have.

    5 · Pediatric, and specifically the proxy-informant problem

    Strong evidence Directions A and B

    No pediatric-specific longitudinal record navigation viewer was found. Pediatric EHR vendors market well-child, immunisation and growth-chart workflows, not cross-institution history navigation.

    On the family side, the intake-AI players are adult. Kahun's own JMIR validation study, 2,550 patients, was aged 16 and over with a median age of 35S32. K Health's co-founder is on record on why pediatrics is harder: "you are not speaking to the child"S33.

    An important correction to our own concept 18. We proposed asking the child separately, citing proxy-agreement figures. The instrument evidence says make it additive, not a replacement. A child-only four-item version of the c-ACT performed measurably worse than the full instrument on every metric: internal consistency 0.67 against 0.76, test-retest 0.66 against 0.72, correlation with the ACQ −0.46 against −0.56, and the authors concluded it did not perform to a high enough standard for immediate useS34. The parent items strengthen the instrument. The disagreement between the two accounts is the clinical signal, not the child's answer alone.

    And our two questions are already validated, with specific recall windows. The c-ACT asks the caregiver about nights woken "during the last 4 weeks"S35. TRACK, for under-fives, deliberately uses different windows per item: night awakenings at 4 weeks, rescue medication use at 3 monthsS36. The ACQ uses a 1-week symptom windowS37. Our "nights woken last month" matches c-ACT and TRACK. Our "reliever uses last week" matches the ACQ, not TRACK. Cite which instrument we are borrowing from, or a pulmonologist will ask.

    6 · Arrived but never attached, with an owner and a deadline

    Strong evidence Direction C

    Every incumbent stops one step short of Direction C, and the gap is documented in each of their own sources.

    The regulator tracks receipt, not filing. CMS quality measure #374, "Closing the Referral Loop", measures whether the referring clinician receives a report. Its own rationale cites an analysis of 103,737 referral scheduling attempts of which only 34.8% resulted in a documented complete appointmentS38. The measure ends at receipt.

    Epic reconciles what arrived, and does not know what should have arrived. Reconcile Outside Information lets a clinician review data from Care Everywhere and MyChart and decide whether to add it to the chart. The documentation contains nothing about tracking expected-but-missing documents, audit trails, or flags for unreconciled outside recordsS39.

    The leading published protocol names the artefacts and assigns nobody. The AMA's STEPS Forward pre-visit planning module enumerates exactly the expected artefacts, including outside provider notes from interval care, and assigns ownership by role. It establishes no owner, no deadline and no tracking mechanism for missing outside records, saying only that staff can call that office to obtain the informationS40. Direction C's entire thesis is the documented hole in the field's own best practice.

    And the failure is measured elsewhere too. A systematic review of failure to follow up test results found abnormal laboratory results not followed up in 6.8% to 62% of cases and radiology results in 1.0% to 35.7%, with the authors noting the true rate is likely underestimatedS41.

    What this changes before Thursday

    The Initial Solution Concept is due 6 Aug and the ideation file says to write it off Direction A. This scan does not change that recommendation. It changes the claim Direction A makes, and it raises Direction C's priority.

    Change the claim

    Stop leading on speed and on click-to-source. Both are occupied and the measured headroom on speed is about eighteen secondsS18. Lead on completeness that is honest about its own limits: the one route plus the named list of what it could not reach. That is a claim no funded product makes, it answers the most-cited barrier in the literature, and it is the bridge from A into B and C.

    Concretely, the one-sentence concept becomes closer to: we help a pediatric pulmonologist reach every accessible piece of a child's respiratory history in one route, and see a named list of what could not be reached, without anything being summarised or concluded.

    Differentiate

    Four positions to hold: the boundary map, the recorded "I don't know" backed by HL7 ASKUS25, the receipt, and refusal to summarise. All four are unoccupied, all four follow from our own insights, and three of the four have real evidence behind them.

    Achieve parity, do not build

    Record aggregation, click-to-source, chronological imaging and acronym expansion are all solved or nearly solved. Visage's Study Navigator has offered chronological federated imaging history with automatic prior display since 2015S42, so the X-ray reel is not novel to radiology, only to a pulmonologist who does not live in the PACS. Frame it that way. Acronym expansion is validated at 95.7% on MIMIC-III, exceeding board-certified physicians in the same comparison, with code and a 3,758-abbreviation dictionary released and no commercial deployment foundS43. That is a build-on, not a build.

    Raise Direction C

    C came third in ideation on the grounds that it was cheap. On this scan it is also the best-evidenced gap: the regulator stops at receipt, Epic stops at reconciliation, the AMA protocol names the artefacts and assigns nobody. It is still one afternoon with the nurse and no build. It should be argued as a co-equal finding, not a parallel freebie.

    The Israeli reading

    Ofek and Eitan are infrastructure, not competitors, and the gap is by definition what sits outside them. The 2014 Knesset review of Ofek is explicit that the private medical sector, independent physicians and private hospitals do not participateS1. That is a 2014 document and we should date it rather than present it as current. Meanwhile 88% of Israeli adults hold supplementary or commercial cover on top of the basket and around 20% of health spending is out of pocketS2, which is the best available proxy for how much care is bought privately and therefore sits outside the exchange.

    None of the four HMO apps offers a general pre-visit clinical history questionnaire. Clalit, Maccabi, Meuhedet and Leumit are all view, book and transact surfaces. The only questionnaires found were Maccabi's teledermatology triage and Clalit Connect's monitoring plansS3. That absence is our white space and it is well evidenced.

    Two live Israeli developments change what is buildable and neither existed two years ago. The Medical Information Mobility Law was approved on 24 July 2024, and its FHIR-based certification programme has a Phase 1 deadline of 26 November 2026 and Phase 2 in March 2027S4. And the Innovation Authority and Ministry of Health opened a regulatory sandbox for high-autonomy medical AI, with submissions closing 14 April 2026 and decisions at the end of JuneS5. Both belong in the concept's feasibility section.

    Monitor

    Four things worth a line in the final presentation. Epic's Art roadmap, which dates contextualised AI summaries and "chat with Art" to November 2026S11. The UCLA randomised trial of Epic's generative chart summariser, protocol published February 2026, results pending, which will be the first randomised evidence in this spaceS44. Ambience's expansion into inpatient and ED. And the Israeli certification deadline in November 2026, which is the first date a patient-initiated pull of a child's record becomes a statutory right rather than a negotiation.

    What this brief could not verify

    Whether Ofek or Eitan reach Terem urgent care, private imaging institutes, private sleep labs or school health services in 2026, which is exactly the boundary Direction A wants to map. Eitan's current coverage, whose newest hard figure is from 2022. Schneider's own pre-visit process and patient portal, because every relevant page on schneider.org.il refused automated access and needs opening by hand. And whether any Hebrew-language pre-visit intake product exists, where we found nothing but cannot prove absence. These are named so the team does not re-research them and does not assert them.
