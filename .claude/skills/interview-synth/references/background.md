# Background — evidence base for synthetic UX interviews

This file backs the guidance in `SKILL.md`. Read it when you want the "why" or need to
cite the research.

## The core finding: grounding beats profiles

Nielsen Norman Group's synthesis of three studies on AI-simulated behavior found that
**"digital twins"** — models grounded in a real person's own interview transcripts or
prior responses — predict that person's answers far better than **"synthetic users"**
built only from a demographic profile. Twins reached ~78–80%+ accuracy on
backfilling/survey tasks and near-perfect correlation (r ≈ 0.98) with human data at the
*population* level; demographic-only synthetic users captured only directional trends and
missed effect magnitudes. Crucially, transcript-grounded twins also **reduced political
bias by ~36–62% and racial bias by ~7–38%** versus demographic models. Takeaway for this
skill: always ground in real material; a profile-only persona is the weakest, most biased
form.

## The documented failure modes

- **Sycophancy / agreeableness.** AI participants tend to agree, rate concepts too
  positively, and "succeed" at tasks real users struggle with — so they never surface the
  friction that real research is for.
- **Low variability / homogenization.** Synthetic responses cluster near the average and
  under-represent the range of real attitudes and behaviors, which is exactly where edge
  cases and the most useful insights live.
- **Shallow, generic output.** Long undifferentiated lists of plausible needs, without
  priority or the contextual "when / why / how" that shapes real decisions.
- **Demographic skew.** Accuracy varies by group; models predict well-documented
  populations better, so niche or under-documented segments are where fabrication is worst.

## When NN/g says synthetic users are acceptable

Only for preliminary work: desk-research synthesis, hypothesis generation, piloting a
study / learning a new domain, and proto-personas to be refined. Explicitly **not** for
replacing real research, validating concepts, making decisions, researching niche
populations, or presenting synthetic output as real findings.

## The responsible-use throughline

Across the sources the same three rules recur, and they are the backbone of this skill:
treat synthetic output as **testable hypotheses**, be **transparent** about its synthetic
origin, and **complement — never replace** — human-centered research. Human validation
remains essential both for uncovering genuinely new behavior and for ethical integrity
(including consent when real people's data is used to build a twin).
