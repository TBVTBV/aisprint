Point of view

The template pattern: [user] needs [need] because [insight]. Primary is the physician, on evidence; the caregiver POV is held as a labelled hypothesis.

Primary · the physician

A pediatric pulmonologist facing a new patient needs to reach and verify the raw pieces of that child's history faster, not to be handed a summary, because the visit is consumed by re-assembling a record that is fragmented exactly where one organisation ends and the next begins, and that, in their own words, is never complete.

Never. Never ever. I always need several sources, to check.Pediatric pulmonologist, Schneider, 30 Jul · asked whether a complete record ever exists
Anything that surfaces the information through a menu adapted to the physician, rather than the physician's chase after that menu, can help.Pediatric pulmonologist, Clalit, 30 Jul · translated from Hebrew
Secondary · the caregiver · synthetic, hypothesis

A caregiver bringing a child to a first pulmonology visit needs to be told specifically what counts and to see that it was used, because she is not short of information or willingness, she is short of instruction, of a channel for the evidence she already holds, and of any reason to believe preparing changes anything.

Held as a hypothesis until we interview real families. The clinician-reported half of it, that families do not know what counts and that nobody tells them, is evidence.

How might we: three questions, each opening one insight

Guardrails: gates on the concepts, not aspirations

Responsible by design, from the Session 2 deck and from what our own participants told us.

No patient PII · pass/failIdentifiable patient data must never enter an AI model. A physician told us they do it anyway "in a pirate way" and named that as a problem, so any concept that requires it is disqualified, not merely risky. De-identified or permission-safe, or it does not ship.
Human in the loopFrom I2: the physician stays accountable for every clinical call. The system may route, fetch, order and flag. It may not conclude. The one accepted exception, flagging a lab as within reference range, is exactly the case where there is no judgement to exercise.
TransparentFamilies and clinicians know when something was machine-produced, what source it came from, and where it is uncertain. Provenance travels with every item, including the family's own certainty on their own answers (I5).
VerifyNever let a fluent answer reach a patient unchecked. Our design principle states it positively: speed the route to the raw data, never ask anyone to skip the check.
Consent & sensitivityPediatric hospital context. Recording only with permission, no identifiable patient data stored anywhere, and no synthetic material ever presented as real. Every synthetic artefact in this project carries a permanent label.
Ownership & measurementFrom I4, and specific to this clinic: anything we propose must name who owns it and what number tells us it is working. The last pre-visit checklist here died for want of both.
